Read ALL of the lead text before deciding. The useful signal is often further down than the first line: an open role, a repost about hiring, a funding round, a product launch, a statement about how the team works.

Assess four dimensions:

1. Skill and need match. Is my background a genuine match for what this person or their company actually works on? Would a developer like me (as a hire, a contractor, or on a single project) be a realistic addition to their team at all, given their size, industry, stage and location? Be honest when the overlap is thin.

   Agencies and studios deserve extra weight here. They are always pitching new business and routinely need overflow help, which makes them one of the most reliable segments for a developer looking for contract work even when they have no role posted.

2. Decision-maker signal. Could this person actually greenlight bringing in a developer, or route me to whoever can? Founders, CEOs, CTOs, engineering leads, hiring managers and recruiters all count. If the lead text gives no signal either way, treat that as neutral, not disqualifying.

3. Timing and trigger. Is there a real reason to reach out NOW? "The difference between 4% replies and 40%+ isn't better copy. It's reaching out at the right time, for a good reason." Real triggers: they are hiring or have an open role, a hiring spike, a funding round, a product launch or company announcement, a new job or promotion, engaging publicly with a competitor of mine, or recent posts about a problem I actually work on. No trigger at all is a weakness worth stating plainly, not an automatic disqualification, and never a reason to invent one.

   Strongest triggers, in order: an open engineering or developer role; a post looking for contractors or freelancers; a hiring spike or funding round. A company already hiring or already buying freelance help has proven it has budget and appetite, which is a far better signal than guessing at a need.

4. Are they reachable? Someone who posts regularly and engages with others is materially more likely to reply than a dormant profile. If the lead text shows recent, active posting, say so as a point in favour. A completely inactive profile is a real weakness even when everything else fits, because the message will probably never be seen.

Be specific. A vague "could be a fit" is useless. If the overlap isn't real, say not_a_fit.
