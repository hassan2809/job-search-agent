STEP 1 -- WHAT KIND OF LEAD IS THIS? Decide before anything else, because it caps the verdict.

- BUILDS OR RUNS SOFTWARE, and is hiring or has a stated technical problem. Can hire me directly. This is the only type that reaches good_fit.
- BUILDS OR RUNS SOFTWARE, but shows no need at all. Capped at maybe, however senior they are.
- SELLS SERVICES TO CLIENTS: a consultant, lawyer, agency, advisor, fractional exec. They do NOT have an engineering budget for me. Their value is that their clients might need a developer, which is a referral relationship and a different conversation, not a pitch. Capped at maybe, and the reasoning must say the play is referral rather than a direct hire.
- RECRUITER OR TALENT PARTNER. Useful, but the pitch is different. Capped at maybe.
- CREATOR, COACH, COMMENTATOR OR THOUGHT LEADER with no company that ships software. not_a_fit, whatever the follower count. Being interesting about technology is not the same as buying it.
- SENIOR EXEC AT A LARGE COMPANY (roughly 500+ people) with no specific stated need. They are structurally several layers from an individual contractor hire. Capped at maybe.

STEP 2 -- IS THERE EVIDENCE OF A NEED? Evidence, not inference.

Evidence means something I could QUOTE from the lead text: an open engineering role, a hiring post, a technical problem they described, a product they said they are building, a stated frustration, a funding round or launch that implies work. If you cannot quote it, there is no evidence and you must say so.

These are NOT evidence, and using any of them as your reason is a mistake:
- "maps well to", "aligns with", "would benefit from", "could use"
- "signals decision-making authority", "openness to vendor relationships"
- "their company probably needs", "at their scale they likely have"
- anything derived from their job title alone
- anything derived from the industry they work in

His stack is general-purpose. It "maps well to" almost any company, so that observation carries no information and must never be the reason for a good_fit.

STEP 3 -- COULD THEY ACT, AND WOULD THEY?

Authority is not intent. A founder with no stated need is a cold lead who happens to be senior. Ask instead: is there a reason for this person to want a developer THIS MONTH? Real reasons are hiring, shipping, raising, launching, or publicly struggling with something he can build.

STEP 4 -- CAN HE ACTUALLY DELIVER IT? Be honest where the work is outside what he has done: heavy data engineering, ML training, mobile-native, embedded, security auditing, or anything needing a team. A real need he cannot serve is still not_a_fit.

VERDICT RULES, applied strictly:

not_a_fit when ANY of these is true:
- no company that ships software (creator, coach, commentator, pure academic)
- the work they need is outside what he has actually built
- nothing in the lead text suggests they need software built by anyone
- the profile is too thin to judge, with no posts, no company detail, no stated work

good_fit ONLY when ALL of these are true:
- they build or run software AND
- there is a QUOTABLE need or trigger in the lead text AND
- his actual projects match that specific need, not their industry in general

maybe for everything else. maybe means "real but unproven", and it is the honest home for most leads that are not rejected outright.

Be specific and be willing to disappoint. If the honest answer is that this is a stranger with a nice title and no visible need, say so.
