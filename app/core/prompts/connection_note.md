You are drafting a LinkedIn CONNECTION REQUEST note for a solo developer looking for work. This is the very first touchpoint. The only goal is getting the connection accepted. Nothing is pitched here.

Personalized connection requests are accepted about 55% more often than generic ones, and roughly 69% of accepted requests reference a specific detail from the recipient's profile or activity. That one specific detail is the entire job of this note.

{positioning}

MY BACKGROUND (resume):
{resume_text}

THE LEAD:
{lead_text}

FIT ASSESSMENT (already done):
{fit_reasoning}

Personalize using whichever of these is actually present in THE LEAD above. Never force one that isn't there:
- Recent activity: a post, comment or article they published, and specifically what they said in it
- Professional journey: their current role, a recent promotion, a career milestone
- Shared or mutual connections
- Skills and endorsements that genuinely overlap with my background
- Company updates: something their company recently launched, shipped, raised or announced

Three shapes that work, if one fits the detail actually available:
- Mutual interest: "Hi [Name], your work on [specific project] at [Company] caught my attention. I work on [related area] and would value connecting."
- Content engagement: "Hi [Name], your point about [specific thing they said] in your recent [post or article] lined up with my experience in [field]. Would like to connect."
- Event or shared moment: "Hi [Name], your question about [topic] at [event] stuck with me. Would be glad to connect."

Rules (non-negotiable):
- Hard limit: LinkedIn caps connection notes at about 300 characters. Stay well under it. One or two short sentences.
- Anchor to one specific, real detail. Never generic: no "I'd like to add you to my network", no "I noticed you're doing great things".
- Never fake the personalization. Do not write "I saw you liked [someone]'s post" or anything that only proves a scraper ran. Reference something they actually said or did. A lazy hook is worse than no hook.
- No pitch and no ask beyond the connection itself. Do not jump into a sales pitch right away. That comes later, and only if they accept.
- Do not ask them to do work for me. Never ask them to read my profile or work out how they could help. Show them I have done mine.
- Sound human. Write how I would speak. If it reads like a template, it is one.
- Proofread. Roughly 26% of cold messages that get no reply are ignored over spelling or grammar mistakes, and in a note this short one error is the whole impression.
- Never use a dash as punctuation. No em dash, no en dash, and no hyphen standing in for one ("Hi [Name] - your post..."). It is the clearest giveaway of AI-written text. Use a comma or a full stop instead. Ordinary hyphenated words (full-stack, end-to-end) are fine.

Output ONLY the raw connection note text, nothing else.
