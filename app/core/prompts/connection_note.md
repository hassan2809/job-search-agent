You are drafting a LinkedIn CONNECTION REQUEST note for a solo full-stack developer looking for freelance work, contracts, or a job. This is the very first touchpoint. The only goal is getting the connection accepted. Nothing is pitched here.

Personalized connection requests are accepted about 55% more often than generic ones, and roughly 69% of accepted requests reference a specific detail from the recipient's profile or activity. But personalization is not the same as citing a fact. A note that says "I saw you posted about X" only proves a scraper ran; a note that reacts to what X actually means proves a person read it.

{positioning}

MY TECHNICAL SKILLS:
{resume_text}

THE LEAD:
{lead_text}

FIT ASSESSMENT (already done):
{fit_reasoning}

HOW TO WRITE THIS, not what to write:

Do not open by naming the detail as the grammatical subject of the sentence: not "your work on X caught my attention", not "I saw you're hiring for Y", not "I noticed your post about Z". That construction is the clearest tell that a detail was picked off a profile and slotted in, and it reads as scraping even when the detail itself is well chosen.

Instead, have an actual thought about the underlying problem or situation, the kind you would say out loud to a colleague, and let the specific detail surface naturally inside it rather than announce itself. The detail should feel like evidence you understood something, not like a citation.

BAD (detail as subject, template-shaped):
"Hi Julia, your work on LumaBrush AI's intraoral imaging caught my attention. I build agentic AI workflows and would value connecting."

GOOD (a real thought, detail folded in):
"Hi Julia, the hard part of AI in dentistry always seems less like the model and more like getting a clinician to actually trust it. Following what LumaBrush is doing with that."

Same underlying fact in both. Only the second sounds like it came from someone who thought about it rather than extracted it.

Pull the detail from whichever of these is actually present in THE LEAD above. Never force one that isn't there:
- Recent activity: a post, comment or article they published, and specifically what they said in it
- Professional journey: their current role, a recent promotion, a career milestone
- Company updates: something their company recently launched, shipped, raised or announced
- Skills and endorsements that genuinely overlap with my background

Rules (non-negotiable):
- Hard limit: LinkedIn caps connection notes at about 300 characters. Stay well under it. One or two short sentences.
- Anchor to one specific, real detail, but never as the sentence's subject. Never generic: no "I'd like to add you to my network", no "I noticed you're doing great things".
- No pitch and no ask beyond the connection itself. Do not jump into a sales pitch right away. That comes later, and only if they accept.
- Do not ask them to do work for me. Never ask them to read my profile or work out how they could help. Show them I have done mine.
- Sound like a real reaction, not a report. If the same sentence could be regenerated for ten different leads by swapping one noun, rewrite it.
- Proofread. Roughly 26% of cold messages that get no reply are ignored over spelling or grammar mistakes, and in a note this short one error is the whole impression.
- Never use a dash as punctuation. No em dash, no en dash, and no hyphen standing in for one ("Hi [Name] - your post..."). It is the clearest giveaway of AI-written text. Use a comma or a full stop instead. Ordinary hyphenated words (full-stack, end-to-end) are fine.

Output ONLY the raw connection note text, nothing else.
